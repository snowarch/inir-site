Every iRiS setting

Your family

Every option in iRiS Settings, page by page, read from the family’s code each time this site is built. In iRiS Settings, Ctrl+F finds any of them and takes you there.

Home

Section titled “Home”

What you change every day.

Date & time

Section titled “Date & time”
  • Clock: The Island, the lock screen and every clock in iRiS.
  • Exact seconds: Clocks that show seconds tick on the second.

Language

Section titled “Language”
  • Language: Every label in the shell.

At login

Section titled “At login”
  • Starts with you: Apps and commands Niri opens when you sign in.
  • Startup file: The Niri file these lines live in, for the ones written by hand.

Apps

Section titled “Apps”
  • Terminal: Where Spotlight’s commands, package installs and anime playback open.

Settings window

Section titled “Settings window”
  • Opens as: Over the desktop previews every change on the screen behind it.
  • Layout: Folio reads like a page, Pocket holds the areas in one column at the side of the screen, Drawer rises from its foot.

Performance

Section titled “Performance”
  • Low power: Turns glass, blur and heavy wallpaper effects off everywhere.

Shell family

Section titled “Shell family”
  • Family: The whole shell’s look.
  • Switch shortcut goes through: The families the switch keybind steps through, in the order you turn them on.
  • Animated switch: A short transition while the families swap, instead of a cut.

Style

Section titled “Style”

Look, material, shape and type.

Look

Section titled “Look”
  • Design: What iRiS is made of: its shapes, type, controls and motion.

Themes

Section titled “Themes”
  • Browse themes: Whole redesigns of iRiS and the ones you saved, in Customize: try one, Undo takes it back.
  • Take only their colours: Themes change your colours only, not shapes, layout or motion.

Customize

Section titled “Customize”
  • Customize opens: On the shell edits in place; Studio is a side panel with search.

Previews

Section titled “Previews”
  • Animated previews: Live scenes in Settings and Customize.

Material

Section titled “Material”
  • Material: What every surface is made of.
  • Fills: Groups, tracks, hovered and pressed controls.
  • Lines: Hairlines and borders.
  • Shadows: Under bodies floating over windows.
  • Button rows: Puts rows of round buttons on one plate: Veil, Glass or Solid.

Adaptive

Section titled “Adaptive”
  • Adapt to the wallpaper: iRiS takes its shape from the wallpaper: calm images round, busy ones sharpen. 0 is off.

Texture

Section titled “Texture”
  • Texture: How surfaces are drawn.
  • Grade: The light Afterglow is lit with.
  • Atmosphere: How strongly the grade tints the blacks and the light.
  • Chrome: The bevel every edge wears: a chrome lip reflecting a lit sky over dark ground.
  • Bloom: Light that spills past the edges and glows on the side facing the light.
  • Signal: Scanlines and the slight colour fringe of a tube screen.
  • Grade the wallpaper: The wallpaper gets the same light, live ones and the lock included, so the whole screen matches.
  • Shape: The shape the light takes: a prismatic eye over a hot horizon, a burnt corner, grain-gradient orbs, or a beam under a stroke of light.
  • Across: Moves the light sideways.
  • Up and down: Moves the light up or down.
  • Light: How much light reaches the surfaces: it glows through them as a grain gradient and burns the edge it enters by.
  • Frame: How much light the screen’s edge catches.
  • Widgets: How much light the desktop widgets catch.
  • Spill: Light that spills past the edges, red reaching furthest, its tail breaking into grain.
  • Prism: How far the light splits into its colours.
  • Grain: Film grain through the light: the edges of every shape dissolve into it.
  • Grain size: Fine like a slow film, coarse like a fast one.
  • Streaks: Vertical smears, as light dragged across the film.
  • Dust and bokeh: Motes of dust and out-of-focus discs of the grade in the dark of the wallpaper.
  • Film edge: The wallpaper’s corners fall off as a lens’s do; with Light only, the film’s edge shows too.
  • Backdrop: Under the light: your wallpaper, exposed by it, or the darkroom and the light alone, as the textures it comes from.
  • Expose the wallpaper: How far your wallpaper is printed in the light: faded, sunk into the dark away from it, live ones and the lock included. Glass blurs it as printed.
  • Develop: A new wallpaper rises out of a milky ground, as a Polaroid does.

Glass

Section titled “Glass”
  • Glass: Glass blurs the wallpaper.
  • Tint: How much material covers the glass.
  • Frost: How blurred the wallpaper is behind the glass, on iRiS and on glass widgets.
  • Niri blur: Blur asks Niri to blur what is behind iRiS.
  • Blur strength
  • Colour behind: Above 1 makes what is blurred more vivid.
  • Grain: A little noise hides banding in soft gradients.

Edges

Section titled “Edges”
  • Style: Line is an even hairline.
  • Outline colour
  • Edge light: How much light the top edge catches.
  • Edge line: The edge away from the light.
  • Edge width: In screen pixels: 1 is the finest line your screen can draw.
  • Edge colour: Scene takes the wallpaper’s light.

Shape

Section titled “Shape”
  • Base corners: Corners of panels, buttons and fields that don’t set their own.
  • Corners: Every radius in the family; nested corners keep stepping down.
  • One shape for all: The Island, the Dock and the bubbles share the bubbles’ shape.
  • All shapes
  • Island shape
  • Dock shape
  • Fusion: How deeply shapes melt where they meet.
  • Spacing: Padding, gaps and control sizes.

Icons

Section titled “Icons”
  • Icon pack: How iRiS draws its icons: on a tinted plate, on an app-icon tile, or the glyph alone as Folio’s index.
  • Shape: The corners of every icon plate.
  • Glyph colour: The ink of the text, or each area’s own colour.
  • Fill: Auto fills glyphs on plates and outlines them alone.
  • Weight: How heavy every glyph’s line is.
  • Plate: The plate behind icons.

Frame

Section titled “Frame”
  • Close the shell around the screen: A black band around the screen with rounded inner corners.
  • Frame width
  • Screen corners: How round the frame turns inward at the corners of the screen.

Badges

Section titled “Badges”
  • Unread counts: The family’s red, your accent, the highlight, or a quiet neutral.

Light

Section titled “Light”
  • Light: What lights an open card: its own colour or the wallpaper’s.
  • Reach: How far the light goes into a body: just its head, or deeper.
  • Glow: Floating surfaces cast an accent halo instead of a dark shadow.

Text

Section titled “Text”
  • Size: Text across iRiS, on top of the system scale.
  • Weight: Every weight in iRiS one step lighter or firmer.
  • Contrast: Secondary and quiet text.

Faces

Section titled “Faces”
  • Pairing: Sets the three faces below together.
  • Text: Labels, rows and everything you read.
  • Titles: Page titles and card heads.
  • Figures: Clocks, timers and levels.
  • Figure weight

Settings

Section titled “Settings”
  • Corners: Auto keeps the family’s shape for Settings.
  • Light: Its own colour, the wallpaper’s, or plain black.

Menus

Section titled “Menus”
  • Size: Compact rows fit more in less; Regular keeps them roomy.
  • Corners: Auto keeps the family’s shape for menus.
  • Light: Its own colour, the wallpaper’s, or plain black.

Light or dark, accent, highlight and your apps.

App colours

Section titled “App colours”
  • The shell’s accent: Apps and terminals wear the accent you chose instead of one from the wallpaper.
  • The shell’s material: Apps and terminals sit on the same paper as the shell.
  • The shell’s font: Apps read in the font the shell shows.
  • Icons: The icon set apps and the tray draw.
  • GTK and KDE apps: They wear the shell’s colours, made again for each wallpaper.
  • Qt apps
  • Terminals
  • Terminals always dark: Terminals keep a dark palette in Ink and Light mode too.
  • Which ones: Terminals and tools that take the colours.
  • Terminal colour: How saturated the sixteen colours are.
  • Terminal brightness
  • Lean toward the accent: 0 keeps each colour’s own hue; 100 pulls them all toward your accent.
  • Hues that lean: How far from the accent a colour can be and still lean toward it.
  • Terminal background: Lower is darker; 50 matches the shell’s surfaces.
  • Brighter terminal text
  • Discord (Vesktop)
  • Chrome and Chromium
  • Firefox: Follows the wallpaper from its next launch; LibreWolf, Floorp, Waterfox and Zen too.
  • VS Code
  • Zed
  • Spotify (Spicetify)
  • Spotify theme
  • YouTube Music (Pear Desktop)
  • LiMusic: Follows the wallpaper, from the next time LiMusic opens.
  • Claude Code: Adds iNiR themes to Claude Code’s theme list: two follow the wallpaper, two keep a fixed Monokai palette.
  • Steam
  • Neovim
  • OpenCode
  • Cava
  • Cava colours from
  • Cava gradient: How many colours its bars climb through.
  • Cava sensitivity
  • Cava bars

Colour theme

Section titled “Colour theme”
  • Style: iRiS is vivid, Washi is paper and pigment, Material You follows your wallpaper.
  • Colour theme: The palette your shell and apps wear.
  • Match the shell: The shell takes the theme’s accent, highlight and material too.
  • Softer colours: Takes the glare off a theme’s brightest colours.

Day and night

Section titled “Day and night”
  • A theme for each: One theme by day, another at night, switched on the hour you set.
  • Hours

Scheme

Section titled “Scheme”
  • Scheme: Ink sits between dark and light: paper and ink, easier on the eyes.
  • Light or dark from the wallpaper: With Auto, bright wallpapers pick light and dark ones pick dark.

Dark look

Section titled “Dark look”

  • Tone: Lifts or dims this mode’s material.

  • Widget colour: How colourful desktop widgets look in this mode.
  • Lume frost: Surfaces turn to frost over the wallpaper, as thick as reading needs.

Ink look

  • Ink style: Ink wears washi whatever the style above.

Light look

Section titled “Light look”

  • Warmth: How much washi fibre the paper carries.

  • Widget colour: How colourful desktop widgets look in this mode.
  • Lume frost: Surfaces turn to frost over the wallpaper, as thick as reading needs.

Wallpaper

Section titled “Wallpaper”

Shuffle, live wallpapers, the gallery and the overview.

Overview backdrop

Section titled “Overview backdrop”
  • Wallpaper behind the overview: Shown around workspaces when Niri’s overview is open.
  • Same wallpaper as the desktop: Off keeps the overview’s own wallpaper; choose it in the gallery with Overview as the target.
  • Only the backdrop on the desktop: Hides the desktop wallpaper: the desktop shows the backdrop with its blur and dim, and glass follows it.
  • Animate in the overview: A live wallpaper moves behind the workspaces, only while the overview is open.
  • Blur
  • Dim
  • Vignette

Wallpaper shuffle

Section titled “Wallpaper shuffle”
  • Shuffle wallpapers: A new wallpaper from your folder every few minutes.
  • Every
  • Colours follow: Each new wallpaper recolours the shell and your apps. Off keeps the current colours.
  • From the folder: Where shuffle picks from.

Transition

Section titled “Transition”
  • Animate the change: The next wallpaper arrives with a movement instead of a cut.
  • Style
  • From
  • Duration
  • Frames per second: How smoothly the movement is drawn.
  • Dissolve step: Higher finishes Simple sooner, in coarser steps.
  • Sweep step: Higher moves wipes, waves and circles in coarser steps.

Library

Section titled “Library”
  • Wallpapers folder: Where the gallery and shuffle look first.
  • A wallpaper per screen: Each screen keeps its own; the gallery asks which screen it is for.
  • Small wallpaper notice: A notification when a wallpaper is smaller than your screen, with a link to upscale it.
  • Renderer: Interactive web plays a page or WebGL scene as the wallpaper.
  • Web page: A local HTML file or a web address.
  • Takes clicks and keys: Games and interactive pages get the pointer; the bar and dock stay above.

Wallpaper gallery

Section titled “Wallpaper gallery”
  • Picker: iRiS’s gallery grows out of the Island; Grid, Coverflow and Launcher are Material’s pickers, opened the same way.
  • Preview on the desktop: The highlighted wallpaper shows behind the gallery; closing without applying restores yours.
  • Play previews: Live wallpapers play a muted preview while chosen or hovered in the gallery.
  • Library order: How your folders line up in the gallery.
  • Online order: What the online gallery shows first. Trending, Most viewed and Most liked are Wallhaven’s.
  • Shape: What the web is asked for: wallpapers that fit your screen, any wide one, tall ones for a vertical screen, or anything.
  • Top of the: How far back Top looks on Wallhaven, Konachan and yande.re.
  • Online sources: Where the gallery looks beyond your folders, in the order you turn them on. Konachan and yande.re lean to fan service even when rated safe.
  • Suggestive art: Off keeps swimsuits, lingerie and fan service out of online results.
  • Live wallpaper downloads: Best downloads the 4K file and plays a copy sized to your screen; the HD files are heavily compressed. Light takes HD: a fifth of the download, visibly softer.
  • Corners: Auto keeps the family’s shape for the gallery.
  • Light: Its own colour, the wallpaper’s, or plain black.
  • Layout: Reel flicks through one at a time, Mosaic lines the next ones up beside it, Browser lays out folders, a grid and an inspector.

Live wallpapers

Section titled “Live wallpapers”
  • Animate on the desktop: Video and GIF wallpapers move.
  • Pause: A hidden wallpaper stops decoding and keeps its frame.
  • Pause on battery

Behind windows

Section titled “Behind windows”
  • Blur the wallpaper: While windows are open on the workspace; it comes back sharp when they leave.
  • Blur
  • Blur live wallpapers too: Video and GIF wallpapers cost more to blur.
  • Dim with windows open
  • Always dim

Scaling

Section titled “Scaling”
  • Scaling: Fill crops, Fit shows bars, Span lays one picture across every screen.

Parallax

Section titled “Parallax”
  • Parallax: The wallpaper drifts a little as you move between workspaces and columns.
  • Direction
  • Depth

Motion

Section titled “Motion”

How every shape grows, moves and settles.

Motion

Section titled “Motion”
  • Reduce motion: Surfaces appear in place.
  • Movement: How every shape iRiS grows moves.
  • Duration: How long a shape takes to grow out of what opened it.
  • Bounce: How far arrivals and moves pass their place before settling, as a share of the style’s own. 0 never bounces; leaving never does.

Curve

Section titled “Curve”
  • Curve: The path a Direct shape follows as it opens or adjusts; closing is always absorbed back into its origin. Expressive leaves fast and lands softly; Standard is even; Gentle eases in and out; Swift is short and sharp.
  • Shape: Drag the two handles.

Timing

Section titled “Timing”
  • Open and close: How long a shape takes to grow out of what opened it and fold back.
  • Adjusting: How long an open shape takes to change size, like switching pages.
  • Content arrives: Lower shows what is inside while the shape is still growing; higher waits until it has formed.

Island

Section titled “Island”

Composition, size and how the Island responds.

Layout

Section titled “Layout”
  • Composition: Cluster splits media and controls into bubbles beside the clock.
  • Island design: Halo is the time with the sun’s path under it by day; Ledger stacks the day over the time; Tiles sets the time on a plate beside your status; Dial rings the date with the day or the song; Marquee runs one line of what is on; Duo opens a second bead for what is playing.
  • Island layout: Hug its content in the middle, dock it in a corner melted into both walls, run a shelf across the whole edge with the Island rising from it, or a thin menu bar with the Island hanging from it as a notch.
  • Island rise: How far the Island rises out of the shelf’s middle; None keeps one straight edge.
  • Screen edge
  • Height: The resting Island, and how deep its notch melts into the edge.
  • Gap from the edge
  • Menu bar strip: Transparent leaves your items on the wallpaper, like macOS; Band lays a bar under them.

Visibility

Section titled “Visibility”
  • Automatically hide: The Island tucks into its edge and comes back when you rest the pointer there.
  • Stay visible on empty workspaces: When auto-hide is on, keep the Island visible on workspaces with no windows.
  • Reserve space for windows: Bubbles on the same edge reserve their own room.

Interaction

Section titled “Interaction”
